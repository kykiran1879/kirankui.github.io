Project: AirBnb Rental Prices

“2017 was a turning point for Airbnb 🏘️—major cities imposed new regulations to combat rising rents and housing shortages 🏙️📉, while public backlash grew over displacement and gentrification 😡. Airbnb’s valuation topped $30 billion 💰, and its launch of “Experiences” expanded tourism into everyday neighborhoods 🌐, raising concerns about infrastructure and community health. Since then, understanding what drives Airbnb prices—like guest reviews ⭐ and minimum stay requirements 📅—has become increasingly important for shaping policy, ensuring fair access to housing, and balancing tourism with local well-being 🧭🏘️.”

🏙️ Project Overview: Predicting Airbnb Rental Prices in NYC

What drives rental prices in New York City? This project analyzes 2017 Airbnb listings to uncover patterns and relationships between:

  • 📍 Borough and neighborhood
  • 🌟 Review scores (1–10)
  • 🛏️ Minimum nights required
  • 💵 Rental price distributions

📊 Data Source

The dataset was obtained from Inside Airbnb, a public data resource.
This analysis uses the NYC dataset as of September 2, 2017, comprising over 40,000 listings across five boroughs.

🧠 Methodology: Supervised Machine Learning

To predict rental prices, I used regression-based supervised learning, focusing on how features like borough, review scores, and minimum nights influence pricing.
Since the target variable (price) is continuous, regression models provided both interpretability and predictive value.

📋 Data Summary

The dataset includes 40,753 rows and 17 columns, each capturing details about Airbnb listings across New York City in 2017.

Key variables include:
- 🆔 id – Unique listing identifier
- 💰 price – Nightly rental price
- 📍 review_scores_location – Location-based review score (1–10)
- 🛏️ room_type – Type of room offered
- 🗨️ number_of_reviews, reviews_per_month – Review activity
- 📆 availability_365 – Number of days available per year

✅ The dataset contains no duplicate records and is ready for analysis.

🔎 Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Before modeling, I conducted an exploratory data analysis to better understand the structure, patterns, and distributions in the dataset. Key steps included:

  • 📈 Visualizing price distributions and identifying outliers
  • 🗺️ Mapping listings by borough and neighborhood
  • 🤝 Exploring relationships between variables like price, minimum_nights, and review_scores_location
  • 📊 Summarizing key metrics by room type and availability

These insights helped guide feature selection and informed model design in the next stage.

📊 Exploratory Results Summary

The distribution of Airbnb listings varies widely across NYC’s five boroughs:

  • 🏙 Manhattan: 19,212 listings — the highest concentration and also the most expensive, with an average price of $180.09
  • 🌉 Brooklyn: 16,810 listings — moderately priced at $119.68
  • 🛫 Queens: Far fewer listings, with pricing trends lower than Manhattan and Brooklyn
  • 🏞 The Bronx: Least expensive borough, averaging $82.83 per listing
  • 🌊 Staten Island: Smaller market with moderate pricing at $128.02

💡 Insight: Manhattan dominates both in quantity and cost, while the Bronx remains the most affordable borough for short-term rentals.

🤖💵 Supervised Machine Learning: Regression Results

In this analysis, we focus on Airbnb listings in Manhattan, which stands out with:
- The highest number of listings
- The highest average rental prices
- And a notable concentration of luxury properties (up to $10,000/night 💰)

This makes Manhattan a compelling case study for exploring how review scores and minimum night requirements influence rental pricing.

🛠️ Methods

We applied a logarithmic transformation to both price and minimum_nights to manage skewed distributions and improve model performance.
- 🔢 review_scores_location was left untransformed, as it already falls within a consistent 1–10 range.

A linear regression model was used to predict the log of rental price using:
- review_scores_location
- minimum_nights (log-transformed)

📈 Regression Output Summary

  • Intercept = 3.238
    • Represents the baseline log(price) when other features are at zero.
  • Coefficient for review_scores_location = 0.173
    • For each 1-point increase in review score, the log(price) increases by 0.173.
    • This translates to roughly an 18.9% increase in actual price.
  • Coefficient for minimum_nights = 0.043
    • For each 1% increase in minimum stay requirement, log(price) increases by 0.043%.
    • The effect is statistically significant but much smaller in practical terms.

🧠 Key Takeaway

⭐ Review scores are a stronger predictor of Airbnb prices than minimum night requirements in Manhattan.
Guests appear willing to pay more for well-reviewed properties, while longer minimum stays have only a modest effect on price.
